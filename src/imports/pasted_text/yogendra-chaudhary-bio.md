Mero Route is a commuter-focused mobile application concept designed to reduce the confusion involved in navigating public transportation across Kathmandu.

**My Role:** UI/UX Designer
**Platform:** Mobile Application
**Tools:** Figma

### The Problem

Public transportation information can often feel fragmented and difficult to understand, particularly for users who are unfamiliar with available routes, vehicle options, stops, or transfers.

### The Goal

The goal was to create an experience that helps commuters quickly understand how to travel from one location to another without overwhelming them with unnecessary information.

• Destination search
• Route discovery
• Transportation options
• Route details
• Navigation guidance

• Frequently used destinations
• Transport information

### Design Focus

I focused heavily on information hierarchy and reducing cognitive load.

What do I need to know before starting the journey?

# My Design Process

I don’t believe every project should follow an identical process.

Different products come with different users, constraints, timelines, goals, and levels of complexity, so I adapt my approach based on what the project actually needs.

The process may change, but the objective remains the same: understand the problem clearly and design the most effective experience possible.

### 01 — Understand

I start by understanding the problem, product goals, business context, users, requirements, and constraints.

Before designing anything, I try to establish what needs to be solved, why it matters, and what success should look like.

### 02 — Research

I gather context around the users, market, competitors, existing solutions, behavioral patterns, and potential pain points.

Depending on the project, this can include desk research, competitor analysis, product reviews, user observations, or discussions with stakeholders.

### 03 — Define

I organize what I’ve learned and translate the findings into clear priorities.

This helps identify the core user problems, product requirements, opportunities, and design principles that should guide the experience.

### 04 — Explore

I explore multiple ways to solve the problem before committing to a final direction.

This stage can involve:

• User flows
• Information architecture
• Journey mapping
• Sketches
• Wireframes
• Layout exploration
• Interaction concepts

The goal is to validate structure and flow before investing heavily in visual detail.

### 05 — Design

I develop the strongest direction into a refined, high-fidelity experience.

I focus on visual hierarchy, spacing, typography, accessibility, responsiveness, consistency, interaction patterns, and overall usability.

Every visual decision should support the experience rather than simply decorate it.

### 06 — Prototype

I connect key screens, flows, transitions, and interactions in Figma to create a realistic representation of the experience.

Prototyping helps me evaluate how the product behaves as a complete journey rather than judging individual screens in isolation.

### 07 — Iterate

Design improves through iteration.

I review feedback, identify friction, challenge assumptions, explore improvements, and refine the experience until the solution feels clearer, more usable, and more intentional.

A design may be delivered, but there is almost always something that can be learned or improved.

## Curiosity

I’m naturally curious about how people interact with digital products and why certain experiences feel effortless while others create friction.

I care about the details that shape the quality of an experience.
